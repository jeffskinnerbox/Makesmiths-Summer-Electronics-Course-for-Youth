
Parents ...
I very much enjoy instructing your children in the Makersmiths' "Summer Electronics Course for Youth"
and I greatly appreciate your participation both during the class and while home.
The kids stayed motivated & focused in class and I attribute this to your efforts.

I concluded the course with the question, posed to you and the children, "Where do we go from here?"
I seeded the discussion with some ideas and I think we came away with the following:

* **Primary Interest**: The kids want to explore some form of coding / programming with the electronics.
  Something beyond [Scratch][01], escaping its limited and highly structured ecosystem,
  and let them continue to experiment with real electronics.
* **Primary Activity:** Ideally an equal combination of coding/screen-time
  and physically assembling a project that does something physical.
  Illustrated simply as:<br>
  **code + breadboards/wires/electrics --> something physical (aka outside the screen)**
* **Concrete Ideas**: Projects using [MicroPython][05] and/or [CircuitPython][06],
  and built with [microcontrollers][02], [discrete components][07] (e.g. transistor),
  with [breakout boards][03] (e.g. distance sensor) and [actuators][04] (e.g. motor).

Industry & academia have a name for this, and they calling it [Physical Computing][08].
Instructing the kids in this domain will need to be much more structured
than my approach used in the course they just finished.
Where in the course just taken, the study materials (i.e. the [book][09]) were optional,
a course in Physical Computing will require some homework on the part of the student.
I can make this easy via an excellent online [video series][10],
but the kids need to view the video in advance and then we work together in the class to get things working.

Also, its important to having a known, common environment they can work in at home and in class.
This would be a single laptop, where all their work will reside.
This common environment will ultimately mean many things,
but one key thing is a common computer platform running Windows 11
with Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL) activated.
I will address configuring WSL in the very first class but creating this common environment,
and bring it to class, is critical.

I welcome your feedback on my proposal above,
and I will be working with my counterparts at MakeSmiths to clarifying how this class could operate,
further definition of the common environment, course content, time frame, etc.

Thank-you all ... Jeff Irland
