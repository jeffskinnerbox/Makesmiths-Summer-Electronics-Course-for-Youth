
Standalone explainer pieces generated with the `/explainer` skill for the **Summer Electronics Course for
Youth** (Makersmiths course code **ELL-W101**). Each answers a single "but *why*?" question in plain
English to deepen student (and instructor) intuition beyond what the experiments cover. They are short
supplementary reading, follow the explainer skill's format (a framing question followed by an accessible
narrative answer), and are *not* bound by the appendix convention used by the syllabus/BOM/lesson plans.


| File | Question it answers |
| :----- | :-------------------- |
| `war-of-the-currents.md` | What Edison, Tesla, and Westinghouse each actually did, and why AC (Westinghouse/Tesla) ultimately won the AC-vs-DC "War of the Currents." |
| `why-do-wires-heat-up.md` | The mechanism that heats a current-carrying wire, and how heat relates to wire size and current (and whether that differs for DC vs AC). |
| `where-does-electricity-flow-in-a-wire.md` | Why DC fills a wire's whole cross-section while AC crowds toward the surface — the "skin effect" and how deep it reaches. |
| `how-fast-is-electricity.md` | Reconciles the very fast random electron speed, the very slow drift velocity, and the near-instant propagation of the electrical signal when voltage is applied. |
| `build-a-lemon-battery.md` | How a lemon battery works, why it needs two different metals (copper + zinc), why a red LED's forward voltage forces cells in series, and why a low-current LED matters — with a materials table and build/test steps for Experiment 5. |
| `electricity-and-computation.md` | Why a relay/switch circuit's real purpose is the on/off *state* it creates (electricity is just the mechanism), the pre-1937 state of ad hoc relay switching, and the story of Claude Shannon's 1937 master's thesis connecting Boolean logic to switching circuits — the intellectual foundation of every digital computer. |
| `maxwells-equations.md` | What Maxwell's Equations are and why they matter — the four rules of electricity and magnetism told twice, once in plain English and once in more detail, ending at the jaw-dropper that light itself is an electromagnetic wave. |
| `dpdt-relay-pinouts.md` | How a DPDT relay works and how to identify its eight pins — why pin layouts differ between brands, and how to map the coil, common, N/O, and N/C contacts yourself with a multimeter's continuity mode. |
| `the-role-of-feedback.md` | How the Class 3 relay oscillator's self-wired output-to-input loop causes continuous oscillation, the theory of operation behind it, positive vs. negative feedback, and how the same measure-compare-correct loop underlies control theory, PID controllers, robotics, and self-driving cars. |
| `rc-networks.md` | Why the Class 4 (Exp 9) capacitor charged fast then slow, the theory of operation of an RC network — the time constant T = R × C and the 63% rule — and how "voltage into time" sets the flash rate and pitch of Experiment 11's oscillator and timed circuits everywhere. |
| `capacitive-coupling.md` | How the Exp 9 LED beyond a capacitor flashed once and faded — a capacitor blocks steady DC but passes a sudden change through its electric field — and why coupling capacitors cross-couple Exp 11's flasher and thread through every audio amplifier. |
| `displacement-current.md` | How a pulse crosses a capacitor's gap when no charge can — Maxwell's displacement current, a changing electric field that acts like a real current — behind the Exp 9 meter spike, and how the same idea predicted electromagnetic waves, radio, and light. |
| `electromagnetism-inventions.md` | How mankind exploited the two-way electricity-magnetism relationship of Classes 5 & 6 (Exp 25, 26, 28) — from Ørsted (1820) and Faraday/Henry induction (1831) through five waves of exploitation — plus a 60-entry timeline of inventions and their societal impact. |
| `magnetic-monopoles.md` | Why a permanent magnet never "runs out" the way a battery does — stored domain alignment, not fuel, destroyed only by heat, shock, or an opposing field — and why poles come in north-south pairs while electric charges exist alone: the missing magnetic monopole. |
| `inductance-and-resistance.md` | Why the Class 5 coil across a 9V battery (Exp 25, 28) isn't a dead short — the transient (current ramps along τ = L/R) vs. steady state (wire + battery internal resistance cap the current), and the inductive kick behind flyback diodes and ignition coils. |
