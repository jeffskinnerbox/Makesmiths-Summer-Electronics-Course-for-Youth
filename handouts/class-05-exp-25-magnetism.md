
# Experiment 25: Magnetism
This is a course on electronics, so what is the relationship between electricity and magnetism?
* **Electricity can create magnetism**:<br>
  When electricity flows through a wire, the electricity creates a magnetic force
  around the wire.
  This principle is used in almost every electric motor in the world.
* **Magnetism can create electricity**:<br>
  When a wire moves through a magnetic field, the field creates a flow of electricity in the wire.
  This principle is used in electric power generation.

**What you will need**<br>
* Large screwdriver or some other iron/steel rod
* 6 feet of 22-gauge wire, or thinner
* 9-volt battery
* Paper clips

**WARNING:** Attach the 9-volt battery briefly to the wire coils.
Leaving the battery on too long will drain or destroy the battery.

## Making an Electromagnet
**Make the Electromagnet**<br>
1. Wind the wire around the shaft of the screwdriver, near its tip.
1. The wire turns should be neat & tight and closely spaced.
   You’ll need to make 100 turns, within a distance of no more than 2 inches.
1. To fit them into this space, you’ll have to make turns on top of previous turns.
1. If the final turn tends to unwind itself, secure it with a piece of tape.
1. Now do these experiments

**100 Turns of Wire**<br>
1. Place a single paper clip on the table.
   How far away from the screwdriver tip must a single paper clip be to **not** be attached to the screwdriver?
1. Using a box full of paper clips, how many paper clips can you pickup?
1. Slide the coil up the screwdriver shaft.
   Now how many paper clips can you pickup?

**50 Turns of Wire**<br>
1. Unwind the coil and cut it in half.
2. Repeat the above steps with 50 turns of wire

**Two 50 Turns of Wire**<br>
1. Place another 50 turns of wire on the same screwdriver
2. Repeat the above steps with two 50 turns of wire on the screwdriver shaft.  Use one battery for both coils.
3. Use two batteries, one for each coil.

Applying a 9-volt battery to a spool of wire seems like a very bad idea,
because you’re going to short out your battery just as you did in Experiment 2.
